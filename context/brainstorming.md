# Brainstorming: the Boredom Meter

## Core idea

> In psychology, boredom isn't laziness—it’s the signal that forces humans to explore. What happens when we give an AI research loop a Boredom Meter?

We are exploring ways to make an AI research loop notice when it is stuck repeating unproductive work, then choose a novel direction to investigate. The goal is to test several concepts and make the result highly visual for a short video demo.

## Directions to explore

### 1. An evolving escape room

- Put one or more agents in an escape room with visible clues, tools, and a clear win condition.
- Give the research loop a Boredom Meter that can detect repetitive or low-progress actions and prompt exploration of a different strategy.
- Let the room or its clues evolve between attempts so viewers can see how the agent learns and adapts.
- Explore a creative, unexpected achievement for the agents—an “Ig Nobel Prize” style award in a playful or biological category.
- Demo potential: show the room, agent actions, progress, boredom level, and strategy changes as they happen.

### 2. A simulated bio lab

- Build a visual lab with multiple tools and simulations for trying biological experiments before spending real-world experiment resources.
- Let multiple agents work in the lab at the same time, propose and run experiments, and share findings.
- Have the Boredom Meter steer agents away from repeatedly running uninformative experiments and toward new hypotheses or methods.
- Make experiment setup, simulated results, agent activity, and the research loop visible in the demo.

## Questions to test

- What should the Boredom Meter measure: repeated actions, low information gain, lack of progress, or some combination?
- What behavior should it trigger—new tools, a new hypothesis, a different experiment, or a change in the environment?
- Can we show a clear before-and-after moment where boredom leads to a useful discovery?
- Which direction gives us a compelling visual demo while still letting us evaluate the research loop?

These are exploratory directions; no concept has been selected yet.
